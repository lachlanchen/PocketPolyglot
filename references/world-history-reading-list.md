# World History Reading List

This note records a high-quality shortlist for Europe, the Middle East, Iran/Persia, Khwarazm/Central Asia, the Ottoman world, Mesopotamia/cuneiform, Egypt, and other great historical dynasties. The goal is not to list everything, but to identify strong first books and a few deeper or more literary options.

## Best First Picks

| Topic | Best First Book | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Europe overall | Norman Davies, _Europe: A History_ | Huge, readable, and less narrowly western-Europe-centered than many surveys. |
| Byzantine / Eastern Roman Empire | Anthony Kaldellis, _The New Roman Empire: A History of Byzantium_ | Best modern full narrative if you want Byzantium as Rome, not just a medieval footnote. |
| Holy Roman Empire | Peter H. Wilson, _The Holy Roman Empire: A Thousand Years of Europe's History_ / _Heart of Europe_ | The serious modern single-volume standard for a very complex empire. |
| Habsburgs | Martyn Rady, _The Habsburgs: To Rule the World_ | Best dynastic story; dramatic, readable, and broad. |
| Ottoman Empire | Marc David Baer, _The Ottomans: Khans, Caesars, and Caliphs_ | Best current first book: Ottoman as European, Middle Eastern, Roman, Islamic, and imperial at once. |
| Middle East modern history | William L. Cleveland and Martin Bunton, _A History of the Modern Middle East_ | Standard balanced textbook-style overview. |
| Arab history | Albert Hourani, _A History of the Arab Peoples_ | Classic cultural and political survey from Islam to modernity. |
| Iran / Persia overall | Michael Axworthy, _A History of Iran: Empire of the Mind_ | Best one-volume readable introduction to Iranian identity, culture, and political history. |
| Achaemenid Persia | Tom Holland, _Persian Fire_ | Best popular narrative for the first Persian world empire and the Greek-Persian wars. |
| Sasanian Persia | Touraj Daryaee, _Sasanian Persia: The Rise and Fall of an Empire_ | Concise specialist account of the last great pre-Islamic Persian dynasty. |
| Khwarazm / Central Asia | S. Frederick Starr, _Lost Enlightenment_ | Best broad context for Khwarazm, Transoxiana, and Central Asia's Islamic golden age. |
| Silk Roads / Eurasia | Peter Frankopan, _The Silk Roads_ | Strong big-picture re-centering of world history around Eurasian routes. |
| Mesopotamia | Amanda H. Podany, _Weavers, Scribes, and Kings_ | Best current general introduction to the ancient Near East through people, cities, and cuneiform records. |
| Assyria | Karen Radner, _Ancient Assyria: A Very Short Introduction_ | Short, expert entry into one of the world's most important early empires. |
| Egypt | Toby Wilkinson, _The Rise and Fall of Ancient Egypt_ | Best readable single narrative for pharaonic Egypt. |

## Very Interesting Dynasties And Empires

- **Achaemenid Persia**: first Persian imperial system, Cyrus, Darius, Persepolis, royal roads, imperial administration. Start with _Persian Fire_; for a more Iranian literary imagination, read the _Shahnameh_.
- **Sasanian Persia**: Rome's great eastern rival, Zoroastrian kingship, late antique world politics. Start with Daryaee.
- **Khwarazmshahs**: short-lived but dramatic Central Asian/Persianate empire destroyed by the Mongols. There is no perfect single popular book only on Khwarazm; read Starr for context, then Mongol histories or _The Cambridge History of Iran, Volume 5_ for depth.
- **Ottomans**: from frontier principality to world empire ruling Anatolia, the Balkans, Arab lands, and Constantinople. Start with Baer; use Caroline Finkel's _Osman's Dream_ for a more traditional detailed chronology.
- **Byzantines / Eastern Romans**: the Roman Empire after Rome, Constantinople, Justinian, icons, Greek fire, Crusades, Ottoman conquest. Start with Kaldellis; use Judith Herrin's _Byzantium_ for a shorter cultural entry.
- **Habsburgs**: strange, dynastic, Catholic, Central European, imperial; marriage diplomacy, Vienna, Austria-Hungary. Start with Rady; use Pieter Judson's _The Habsburg Empire_ for modern social-political interpretation.
- **Assyria and Babylon**: palace reliefs, libraries, deportations, kingship, cuneiform bureaucracy. Start with Podany; add Radner for Assyria and Paul Kriwaczek's _Babylon_ for a popular civilization narrative.
- **Ancient Egypt**: pharaohs, Nile state, pyramids, temples, bureaucracy, religious kingship. Start with Wilkinson; use Ian Shaw's _The Oxford History of Ancient Egypt_ for reference.

## Novel, Epic, And Literary Options

These are useful if you want something that plays the role of _Gone with the Wind_ for a civilization: not always strict history, but emotionally memorable.

| Region | Literary Pick | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Iran / Persia | Ferdowsi, _Shahnameh: The Persian Book of Kings_, trans. Dick Davis | The great Persian epic; kingship, heroes, tragedy, identity. |
| Ottoman Istanbul | Orhan Pamuk, _My Name Is Red_ | Court art, miniatures, murder mystery, Ottoman intellectual world. |
| Ottoman late empire | Jason Goodwin, _The Janissary Tree_ | Accessible historical mystery set in 1830s Istanbul. |
| Balkans / Ottoman-Habsburg frontier | Ivo Andric, _The Bridge on the Drina_ | One of the great novels of empire, borderland, and historical memory. |
| Egypt modern society | Naguib Mahfouz, _The Cairo Trilogy_ | Family saga and modern Egyptian social history. |
| Ancient Rome | Robert Graves, _I, Claudius_ | Classic imperial court novel. |
| Tudor England | Hilary Mantel, _Wolf Hall_ | Probably the best modern English historical novel. |
| Russia / Europe | Tolstoy, _War and Peace_ | Napoleonic Europe through family, war, and historical philosophy. |

## If Buying Only A Few

1. Peter Frankopan, _The Silk Roads_ - one book to connect Europe, Iran, Central Asia, China, India, and the Middle East.
2. Marc David Baer, _The Ottomans_ - one book for Turkey, Balkans, Arab lands, Islam, Europe, and empire.
3. Amanda H. Podany, _Weavers, Scribes, and Kings_ - one book for Mesopotamia, cuneiform, and the early literate world.
4. Michael Axworthy, _A History of Iran_ - one book for Persia/Iran as cultural continuity.
5. Toby Wilkinson, _The Rise and Fall of Ancient Egypt_ - one book for Egypt as a long-lived civilization.
6. Anthony Kaldellis, _The New Roman Empire_ - one book for Byzantium/Eastern Rome.
